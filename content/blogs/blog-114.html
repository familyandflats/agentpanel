Hidden Costs of Buying Property in Gurugram: What Most Buyers Ignore

The advertised price of a property is only the starting point. In Gurugram, the amount a buyer finally spends can also include statutory charges, financing expenses, maintenance deposits, fit-out costs and transaction-specific fees. These items vary by property and buyer, so the safest approach is to prepare an all-inclusive cost sheet before paying a booking amount.

1. Stamp Duty

Stamp duty is a major statutory cost and should not be reduced to a generic '6-8%' rule. Haryana's Revenue Department currently lists sale or conveyance stamp duty at 7% in urban areas and 5% in rural areas, with lower rates for a sale executed in favour of a woman: 5% in urban areas and 3% in rural areas. The applicable treatment depends on the actual instrument, location and parties, so confirm the current amount for the specific transaction through the registration system or Sub-Registrar.

2. Registration Fee and Service Charges

Registration fee is separate from stamp duty. Haryana's registration guidance says buyers can obtain the applicable property value, stamp duty, registration fee and service charges through the HARIS/Sub-Registrar process. Because fees and slabs can change, calculate them using current government information close to the transaction date.

3. GST Where Applicable

GST treatment depends on the nature and stage of the property. CBIC's residential real-estate framework provides effective rates for qualifying construction services, including 5% for residential apartments other than affordable housing and 1% for qualifying affordable housing under the post-2019 scheme, subject to the applicable conditions. Importantly, a completed property transaction where the entire consideration is received after the relevant completion certificate or first occupation is treated differently. Buyers should confirm the exact tax position for the unit instead of assuming that every purchase attracts 5% GST.

4. Preferential Location and Floor-related Charges

Some projects may charge more for a particular view, floor, corner position or other location attribute. The terminology and charging structure are project-specific. Ask for a written cost sheet showing every such component and whether tax is included or additional.

5. Maintenance Deposits and Advance Maintenance

At possession or transfer, a buyer may need to pay advance maintenance, security deposits, common-area charges or other community-related amounts. For resale homes, also check whether the seller has cleared outstanding maintenance and utility dues.

6. Club and Amenity Charges

Club membership or amenity-related charges can be included, optional or separately billed depending on the project and agreement. Do not assume they are part of the base property price. Verify the amount, frequency and refundability where relevant.

7. Parking-related Cost or Rights

Parking treatment can differ by project and transaction. Instead of accepting a verbal statement, review the sanctioned/project documentation and agreement to understand what parking right is being transferred or allotted and whether any amount is separately payable.

8. Home-loan Costs

Financed purchases can involve processing fees, valuation charges, legal review, documentation charges, insurance products and interest during the loan period. Compare the lender's annual percentage impact and one-time fees rather than only the headline interest rate.

9. Brokerage and Professional Due Diligence

Brokerage may apply depending on how the transaction is arranged. Buyers may also choose to pay an advocate, chartered accountant, engineer or other professional for title, tax, valuation or technical review. These are additional costs, but they can reduce transaction risk when the scope is appropriate.

10. Interiors, Repairs and Move-in Cost

A ready home can still require wardrobes, kitchen work, lighting, appliances, painting, air-conditioning, curtains or repairs. For a resale property, inspect the condition of plumbing, electrical systems, flooring and fixed equipment. Premium fit-outs can materially change the total capital invested.

11. Utilities and Ongoing Ownership Cost

Meter deposits, utility connection charges, recurring maintenance, property-related taxes where applicable and insurance can affect the long-term cost of ownership. Investors should include these outgoings when estimating rental yield.

Build an All-inclusive Cost Sheet

Before committing, list the negotiated property price and every additional amount separately: stamp duty, registration, applicable GST, maintenance, amenities, financing, professional fees, brokerage, interiors and moving costs. Ask which figures are fixed and which are estimates. A complete cost sheet makes different properties easier to compare and reduces the risk of a budget surprise at registration or possession.
