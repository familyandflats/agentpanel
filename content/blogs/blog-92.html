13 Real Estate Terms You Must Know Before Investing

Powered By - FamilyandFlats August 23 2025



Property decisions become easier when the language used in brochures, agreements and registration documents is clear. Buyers do not need to become lawyers or valuers, but they should understand the basic terms that affect usable space, legal title, transaction cost and expected income. The following 13 terms are a practical starting point before you compare homes or investment properties in Gurugram.

1. Carpet Area

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, carpet area broadly refers to the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding external walls, service shafts, balconies, verandahs and open terraces, while including internal partition walls. For a buyer, carpet area is the most useful starting point for comparing how much space can actually be used inside different homes.

2. Built-up Area

Built-up area is a market term commonly used for the carpet area plus the thickness of walls and, depending on the project, certain attached areas such as balconies. Because developers may present area definitions differently, ask for the project-specific area statement instead of relying only on a headline number.

3. Super Built-up Area

Super built-up area is another market term that usually adds a proportionate share of common areas such as lobbies, corridors and other shared spaces to the built-up area. It is not the same as carpet area. When comparing price per square foot, first confirm which area basis is being used.

4. RERA Registration

RERA is the regulatory framework intended to improve transparency and accountability in real estate. A buyer considering a project should check the applicable state RERA portal for the registered project details, promoter information, approvals and declared timelines. RERA registration is an important verification step, but it should not replace independent title and document due diligence.

5. Collector or Circle Rate

The collector or circle rate is the government-notified reference value used for property registration and related calculations in a locality. It is different from the negotiated market price. Before completing a transaction, confirm the current applicable rate for the exact property and transaction type.

6. Stamp Duty

Stamp duty is a statutory charge on specified property instruments. The applicable amount depends on factors such as location, instrument and buyer category. Rates can change, so buyers should verify the current amount through the relevant government registration system or Sub-Registrar rather than relying on an old percentage quoted online.

7. Registration Fee

Registration fee is separate from stamp duty and is paid for registering the relevant property document. Buyers should budget for both. The exact registration fee and service charges should be checked at the time of transaction through the applicable Haryana registration process.

8. Agreement to Sell

An agreement to sell records the agreed commercial terms and the parties' obligations before the final transfer. It is not the same thing as the final title document. Buyers should understand payment milestones, conditions, default provisions, document requirements and completion timelines before signing.

9. Sale Deed or Conveyance Deed

The sale or conveyance deed is the principal document through which ownership is transferred, subject to applicable law and registration. Names, property description, consideration, title representations and possession terms should be checked carefully before execution and registration.

10. Occupancy Certificate (OC)

An Occupancy Certificate is issued by the competent authority where applicable after confirming that the completed building satisfies the relevant requirements for occupation. Buyers of completed properties should ask what completion and occupancy documentation applies to that project and unit.

11. Completion Certificate (CC)

A Completion Certificate concerns completion of construction in accordance with sanctioned requirements where applicable. An OC and a CC serve related but different purposes, so buyers should not assume one automatically substitutes for the other.

12. Encumbrance and Title Due Diligence

An encumbrance can include a mortgage, charge, claim or other interest affecting the property. Title due diligence is the broader process of reviewing ownership documents, approvals, encumbrances, litigation risks and transaction history. A buyer should consider independent legal review for the specific property rather than relying only on a broker or brochure.

13. Capital Appreciation and Rental Yield

Capital appreciation is the increase in a property's value over time, while rental yield measures rental income relative to the property's value or cost. Neither is guaranteed. Historical price movement, vacancy, maintenance cost, taxes, transaction costs and local supply should all be considered before treating a property as an investment.

Final Check Before You Buy

Knowing these terms helps you ask better questions, but every transaction still needs property-specific verification. Compare carpet area rather than only brochure area, verify RERA and title documents, calculate statutory charges using current government information, and understand the complete payment schedule before committing funds.
